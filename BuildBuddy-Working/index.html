        Before you buy

        A few things that catch most first-time builders.

        Start with the graphics card

        For games it makes the biggest difference. Decide the resolution and frame rate you want, then pick a card that reaches it.

        Match the processor and the board

        The socket and memory type have to match. Choose one of them first and the list will narrow down to parts that work with it.

        Leave room in the power supply

        Aim for roughly 30 percent more wattage than the estimated peak draw. That keeps the PC calm under load and leaves space to upgrade.

        Measure the case

        Check the longest graphics card and the tallest cooler the case supports. Those two limits cause most surprises at assembly time.

        Read the USB details

        USB-A and USB-C describe connector shapes, not speed. Check the listed Gb/s rate, whether a port is on the rear or front, and whether the case cable has a matching motherboard header.

        Choose storage for the job

        NVMe drives use an M.2 slot and are usually fastest; SATA SSDs offer a simple capacity upgrade, while hard drives are slower but can store large media libraries cheaply. Read speed is a best-case figure, not a game frame-rate boost.

        Cooling and noise go together

        More radiator or heatsink capacity can help with sustained loads, but case clearance, fan placement and noise matter too. Check the exact cooler height or radiator mount before buying.

        Treat estimates as a shortlist

        Prices, power draw, USB layouts and game performance can differ by exact model and revision. Verify the manufacturer specification and current retailer listing before ordering.
